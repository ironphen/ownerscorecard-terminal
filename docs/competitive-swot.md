# The competitive field, and the SWOT

2026-07-10. Deep-research pass (106 agents; every claim below survived 3-vote adversarial
verification against primary sources — competitor pricing pages fetched live, Value Line's
figures read from its own SEC filings, newsletter economics from beehiiv's first-party 2026
report). The owner's thesis under test: "ROIC.ai is obviously far ahead, which doesn't bother
us because our library of research notes over time is our moat."

## The field, verified

The market splits into two camps, and the split is the finding:

**Verdict-issuing** (crowded): GuruFocus ranks every stock 0–100 (GF Score) and tells readers
outright to "invest in companies with high GF Scores." Simply Wall St is maximal-verdict — a
30-check snowflake, automated fair values, explicit "undervalued" calls — and its DCF runs on
sell-side analyst estimates, the methodological opposite of filings-computed records.
stockanalysis.com relays analyst price targets and consensus Buy/Hold/Sell. The verdict end of
the market is saturated.

**Verdict-free data infrastructure** (ROIC.ai's ground): ROIC.ai claims the same
"reader decides" posture in its own copy ("it's up to you to decide"), covers a claimed 60,000+
tickers (marketing count; includes OTC and share classes), prices as an API business
($29–89/mo, tiers defined by endpoints and rate limits — zero mentions of ratings or targets
on the pricing page), and has productized an MCP server feeding Claude/ChatGPT/Cursor (real
capability, no visible adoption: 2 GitHub stars). It still carries live prices and third-party
analyst data, so OSC's stricter doctrine remains distinguishable — but "no verdicts" ALONE is
a commodity claim now.

**Free breadth is a $0 commodity**: stockanalysis.com gives 130,000+ symbols away free —
prices, financials, screeners — and sells depth ($79–199/yr). Its free history stops near 5
years; OSC's free records run 10. Depth-per-company, not breadth, is where the free product
differentiates.

**Value Line, the incumbent whose form we revive, read from its own filings**: publications
revenue ~$24.7M FY2025 and shrinking (−2.9%, accelerating to −3.7% in the following nine
months); publishing operating income down 32.6%; 61% of pre-tax income now comes from its
asset-management trust interest versus 23% from publishing. The paying audience for this form
is real and contracting; the incumbent survives on float-adjacent economics, not subscriptions.

**The economics of a paid notes tier** (the pressure test): investing is the HIGHEST-priced
newsletter vertical (median $27/mo, $292/yr) — and the planned $300–600/yr sits at or above
that median and above Lyn Alden ($249/yr), the strongest solo-author benchmark. Median
free-to-paid conversion: 0.62–0.78%. Top-decile finance publications: 18–20%. Churn (medium
confidence, 2–1 vote): investing ~11.7%/mo and money ~16.7%/mo at the median — the leakiest
verticals there are, with ~6-month median subscriber lifetimes.

## SWOT, in Buffett's terms

**Strengths.** (1) The lowest-cost operator in the category: ~$0/month against competitors
carrying teams and data-vendor contracts — Buffett's structural favor; a revenue level that is
terminal decline for Value Line's cost base is a spectacular business for one person and AI.
(2) Free depth where the free giants stop: ten-year filings-computed records against
stockanalysis's five. (3) The form itself — comparative groupings tables, the Manual, tests
with the arithmetic showing — which no competitor has and which is presentation, not features.
(4) The strictest provenance doctrine in the field (no prices at all, every figure traced),
distinguishable even from ROIC.ai. (5) Permanent URLs as citation equity.

**Weaknesses.** (1) "Verdict-free" alone is not a moat — ROIC.ai already claims it. (2) Zero
brand today, zero published notes today (all four are being hand-written). (3) Coverage breadth
is structurally lost, permanently — 17–37x gaps that must never be chased. (4) The planned
price offers no low-cost cushion: it demands top-tier perceived quality on day one. (5) One
founder.

**Opportunities.** (1) The verdict-free PUBLICATION lane is verifiably empty: nobody sells
hand-written, primary-source, Berkshire-register research notes. (2) Value Line's contracting
~$25M/yr audience is orphaned — its own owner's attention sits with the asset-management trust.
(3) The AI-citation channel is winnable on provenance (our endpoints and llms surfaces) while
ROIC.ai's MCP head start has no traction yet. (4) Top-decile conversion (18–20%) exists in this
vertical — the ceiling is high for genuinely differentiated work.

**Threats.** (1) The newsletter base rates: median conversion under 1% and the worst churn of
any vertical — the moat thesis fails at the median and only works in the top decile.
(2) ROIC.ai owning the AI-data channel if its MCP distribution catches. (3) Value Line's
filings as a memento mori: the form's paying audience shrinks yearly. (4) The institutional
imperative — feature-chasing into screeners/breadth/APIs where competitors' marginal cost is
near zero and ours is attention.

## Strategic implications (the honest read)

1. **"The notes are the moat" half-survives.** The lane is empty — that part is verified. But
   a library is only a moat if it compounds retention into the top decile; at the vertical's
   median the bucket leaks faster than it fills (~6-month lifetimes). The counter-levers are
   already doctrine: annual-first billing, and the permanence of a citable library — a note
   compounds where a newsletter issue expires. The thesis should be restated: *the notes are
   the moat only if the terminal underneath them earns top-decile trust first.*
2. **The defensible wedge is the pairing, not either half**: the free terminal (depth,
   provenance, the form) as the trust engine and funnel; the notes as the monetized scarce
   layer. ROIC.ai owns verdict-free DATA; nobody owns the verdict-free PUBLICATION.
3. **Funnel math sets the sequence**: at median conversion, 50,000 free readers yield ~300–400
   subscribers. Audience precedes paywall; the marketing doctrine (earn citation) is not
   optional garnish, it is the business plan.
4. **What NOT to build, ever**: coverage breadth, screener feature-parity, price feeds, an API
   business with tiers and rate limits, anything where ROIC.ai or stockanalysis.com operate at
   near-zero marginal cost. Their treadmill, their economics.
5. **The scale asymmetry is the quiet edge**: Value Line's dying publication revenue would fund
   this operation ten thousand times over. Compete where the economics structurally favor you —
   here, that is the cost structure itself, which makes patience affordable and the institutional
   imperative resistible.
