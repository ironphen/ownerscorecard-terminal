## I. The call that keeps being wrong

- [S] 1976, at indexing's birth: First Index Investment Trust raised $11.3M against a hoped-for
  $50-150M; Bogle: "an abject failure." Critics: "un-American," "a sure path to mediocrity."
  Fidelity's Edward Johnson: "I can't believe that the great mass of investors are going to be
  satisfied with receiving just average returns." (Zweig 2016 retrospective; Vanguard "50 years,
  50 facts" 2026. The "Help Stamp Out Index Funds" poster story is attested but not documented
  as a dated primary artifact — use with care or drop.)
- [S] Verdict on 1976: $10,000 at end-1976 → nearly $2.0M by 2026-03-31, dividends reinvested,
  net of fees (Vanguard, 2026).
- [S] Bernstein, 2016-08-23, Inigo Fraser-Jenkins: "The Silent Road to Serfdom: Why Passive
  Investing Is Worse Than Marxism." Verbatim: "A supposedly capitalist economy where the only
  investment is passive is worse than either a centrally planned economy or an economy with
  active market led capital management." What followed: S&P 500 TR ~+52.8% in 3 years, +131.7%
  in 5, ~+252% over 2017-2025 (Damodaran annual data, compounded). Grade honestly: wrong on
  investment implication; the systemic claim unresolved. Passive overtook active in US fund
  assets end-2023 (Morningstar, Jan 2024).
- [S] Elliott/Paul Singer, Q2 2017 letter (2017-07-27): "Passive investing is in danger of
  devouring capitalism"; "a blob."
- [S] Burry, Bloomberg 2019-09-04: index funds like "the bubble in synthetic asset-backed CDOs
  before the Great Financial Crisis… price-setting in that market was not done by
  fundamental security-level analysis." What followed: COVID crash within 6 months was NOT a
  passive-liquidity event; index functioned; 2020 finished up. His own timing record: tweeted
  "Sell" 2023-01-31, then "I was wrong to say sell" 2023-03-30.
- [S] Grantham, "Waiting for the Last Dance" (GMO, 2021-01-05): "full-fledged, epic bubble…
  right along with the South Sea bubble, 1929, and 2000." What followed: 2022 delivered -18.0%
  TR, then new highs; 2021-2025 compounded ~+32.7%.
- [S] Einhorn, Bloomberg Masters in Business, 2024-02-08: "I view the markets as fundamentally
  broken"; "Passive investors have no opinion about value." What followed: +24.9% (2024),
  +17.8% (2025); record closes above 7,600 by 2026-06-02.
- [S] THE HONEST EXCEPTION: GMO's January 2000 10-year forecast (-1.9%/yr real) was RIGHT
  (realized ~-3.5%/yr real) — but it was a valuation call on US large caps, not a claim the

## III. Graham's blessing (the canon, verbatim)

- [P] Defensive/enterprising definitions (Intelligent Investor, 2003 HarperBusiness ed. of the
  1973 text): defensive = "avoidance of serious mistakes or losses" + "freedom from effort,
  annoyance, and the need for making frequent decisions." Enterprising = willing "to devote
  time and care to the selection of securities that are both sound and more attractive than
  the average."
- [P] The no-middle-ground passage (Ch. 7, pp. 175-176): "Investment policy… depends in the
  first place on a choice by the investor of either the defensive (passive) or aggressive
  (enterprising) role." (Full quote in sweep.)
- [P] The effort doctrine: "The rate of return sought should be dependent, rather, on the
  amount of intelligent effort the investor is willing and able to bring to bear on his task."
- [P] THE 5-POINT HURDLE: the enterprising investor's work is not worth the effort "unless he
  could hope to add, say, 5% before taxes to the average annual return" on the stock portion.
  (This is the essay's quantitative spine for the sorting AND the letter's reason-to-exist.)
- [P] Graham 1976 (FAJ "A Conversation with Benjamin Graham"): "I am no longer an advocate of
  elaborate techniques of security analysis in order to find superior value opportunities…"
  CRITICAL NUANCE: in the same interview he did NOT tell individuals to index — index-matching
  was his standard for INSTITUTIONS ("the average manager cannot beat the market — a logical
  contradiction"); for individuals he prescribed a highly simplified single-criterion approach.
  "Graham died telling everyone to index" is folklore that overstates; the essay can be more
  precise than the folklore in BOTH directions.
- [S] Samuelson, "Challenge to Judgment" (JPM, Fall 1974): most professionals can't beat the
  market; challenged someone to set up an S&P-tracking portfolio. Ellis, "The Loser's Game"
  (FAJ, Jul-Aug 1975) [P]: the beat-the-market premise "appears to be false."
- [S] Bogle: First Index Investment Trust IPO completed 1976-08-31; $11.3M vs ~$150M hoped
  ("a 93 percent shortfall from our goal").
- [P] Buffett's will (2013 letter): 90% very-low-cost S&P 500 fund (Vanguard suggested), 10%
  short-term governments. "My money, I should add, is where my mouth is."
- [P] The Protégé bet (2007-12-19 → 2017): index fund +125.8% (8.5%/yr); the five
  funds-of-funds: +21.7%, +42.3%, +87.7%, +2.8%, +27.0%. "In every one of the nine years
  [after 2008], the roof fell in."
- [P] Know-nothing vs know-something (1993 letter): "By periodically investing in an index
  fund… the know-nothing investor can actually out-perform most investment professionals";
  the know-something investor concentrates in five to ten.
- [P] Munger: "the wise ones bet heavily when the world offers them that opportunity… the
  rest of the time, they don't"; Berkshire's wealth from ~ten insights.

## IV. The sorting (the essay's destination)

Built from III: the index question exists only on the defensive side of Graham's wall. On the
enterprising side the question is idle — the return comes from work and protocol against the
5-point hurdle, whatever the average does. (No new facts needed; this section is the reading.)
