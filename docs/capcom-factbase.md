## The record [P, from each year's 決算短信]

- FY3/2026: net sales ¥195,365M (+15.2%), operating income ¥75,295M (+14.5%), ordinary income
  ¥74,134M, net income to owners ¥54,587M (+12.7%). Operating margin 38.5%. EPS ¥130.50, ROE
  22.1%, BPS ¥639.03.
- Ten-year sales (¥M): FY16 77,021; 17 87,170; 18 94,515; 19 100,031; 20 81,591; 21 95,308;
  22 110,054; 23 125,930; 24 152,410; 25 169,604; 26 195,365.
- Ten-year operating income (¥M): FY16 12,029; 17 13,650; 18 16,037; 19 18,144; 20 22,827;
  21 34,596; 22 42,909; 23 50,812; 24 57,081; 25 65,777; 26 75,295. Rose EVERY year.
- Operating margin walk: 15.6% (FY16) → 28.0% (FY20) → 39.0% (FY22) → 38.5% (FY26). A
  structural step-up as the mix went digital, NOT just volume growth.
- Net income rose every year across the decade (¥7,745M FY16 → ¥54,587M FY26).
- Streak [P, management-stated]: FY3/2026 = 13th consecutive year of operating-income growth,
  11th straight year of >10% OP growth, 9th straight year of record profit. (10 of the 13 years
  primary-verified year-by-year; the pre-FY2016 years rest on management's count.)
- FY3/2027 GUIDANCE [P, label as forecast]: sales ¥210,000M (+7.5%), OP ¥83,000M (+10.2%), NI
  ¥58,000M, EPS ¥138.65. The +10.2% OP guide conveniently matches the stated "10%+ per year" KPI.
- Segments FY3/2026 (external sales / segment OP, ¥M): Digital Contents 144,277 / 70,618;
  Amusement Facilities 25,656 / 3,201; Amusement Equipment 17,780 / 10,033; Other 7,650 / 3,645.
  Digital Contents = ~74% of sales and ~94% of segment OP. DC segment margin ~48.9% (has run
  high-40s to mid-50s: FY23 54.5%, FY25 52.1%).
- Units: consumer software 59.07M units FY3/2026 (record, +13.9%); Resident Evil Requiem
  (Feb 2026) 6.91M. Digital = 93.0% of units (up from 75.5% FY22). Catalog ≈ 84% of units.
- Balance sheet (Mar 31, 2026) [P]: cash & deposits ¥148,002M; investment securities ¥15,053M;
  total assets ¥339,307M; net assets ¥267,716M; equity ratio 78.8%. Zero bank borrowings (both
  prior-year loans repaid); only ~¥5,097M lease obligations. Net cash ≈ ¥143B (≈¥163B incl.
  investment securities). Fortress.
- Shares [P]: 533,011,246 issued incl. treasury; 114,702,403 treasury (~21.5%); ~418.3M
  ex-treasury. CORRECTION to the premise: the recent split was 2-for-1 effective **April 1,
  2024** (not 2023). Prior 2-for-1 splits April 2018 and April 2021. Treasury cancellation
  between Mar-2022 and Mar-2023. Regular buybacks (discretionary, not a committed program).
- Dividends [P]: payout policy ~30%, recently ~34-35%. FY26 DPS ¥45, payout ~34.5%; FY27 plan
  ¥46. Dividend-on-equity rose 3.1% → 7.6% over the decade. Total FY26 dividends ¥19,179M.
- Market cap [S, aggregator — verify against a live quote]: ~¥3,266 (Jul 6) / ¥3,325 (Jul 7)
  → ~¥1.37-1.39T (~$8.6B at ~¥162/USD). P/E ~25.5x TTM, ~22.4x forward; P/B ~5.2x; EV/EBIT
  ~17x; yield ~1.4%; ROIC ~57% (aggregator-computed). 52-wk ¥2,736-4,631; beta 0.46.

## The moat / catalog economics

- Platinum Titles [P, capcom.co.jp/ir]: 127 titles have each sold >1M units (as of Mar 31,
  2026). Series cumulative: Resident Evil ~201M; Monster Hunter ~127M; Street Fighter ~59M;
  Mega Man ~44M; Devil May Cry ~38M; Ace Attorney ~14M; Dragon's Dogma ~14M.
- Top individual titles [P]: MH World 22.1M (29.6M incl. Iceborne Master Ed.); MH Rise 18.6M;
  RE2 remake 18.3M (best-selling RE ever); RE7 17.4M; MH World: Iceborne 16.0M; RE Village
  14.9M; RE4 remake 13.6M; RE3 remake 13.3M; MH Wilds 11.4M; DMC5 11.2M.
- Remake economics: RE2 remake (18.3M) proves the strategy — rebuild owned IP, assets, lore.
  RE7 dev cost reportedly amortized in ~2 years, then largely profit [S, Famitsu via Kotaku —
  get primary before quoting].
- RE Engine [P, Integrated Report 2025]: proprietary engine; assets "shared across titles";
  developers moved between teams "do not need to relearn the engine." The cost-side moat, in
  Capcom's own words. (The circulating "90% faster testing" figure NOT found in primary — [U].)
- Pricing ladder [P, IR 2025 COO section]: launch ~$60-70, step down over ~5 years toward $10
  then ~$5; recover dev cost in 1-2 years; time cuts to new-title reveals (MH World cut to
  $9.99 at the MH Wilds reveal). This is the mechanism behind catalog durability.

## The pressure-test (the honest holes)

- CONCENTRATION: ~94% of operating profit is one segment (Digital Contents); within it, the top
  of the Platinum list is almost entirely Monster Hunter + Resident Evil. Capcom discloses NO
  franchise-level P&L, so an exact "% from MH+RE" is an estimate — but the unit data makes the
  two franchises clearly dominant, and the "durable catalog" (~84% of units) is itself mostly
  MH+RE. Management itself flags pipeline thinness [P quote]: "We consistently release two to
  three major new titles each year, but recognize the need to grow our pipeline going forward."
- LIVE FRANCHISE-FATIGUE WARNING — MH Wilds: the FY2026 flagship sold ~8M in 3 days (Feb 2025)
  but PLATEAUED at 11.4M (Mar 2026), only +0.4M in the final quarter [P, Capcom's own sequential
  Platinum figures] — well BELOW MH World (22.1M) and MH Rise (18.6M). Steam concurrents
  collapsed ~98% (1.38M peak → <20K); recent reviews "Overwhelmingly Negative" over PC
  performance and design [S, SteamDB]. The most important new release in the flagship franchise
  underperformed its two predecessors and shed players fast.
- ONE-DECADE RECORD: pre-2013 Capcom was genuinely lumpy (OP bounced ¥5.6-14.6B with no trend,
  a sharp FY2009 trough) [S, Annual Report 2015]. The smooth compounding is a ~decade-long
  phenomenon built on the digital/catalog shift — a shift now largely spent (digital already
  93% of units), so that specific tailwind is mostly behind, not ahead.
- FX FLATTERY: ~84% of units sold overseas, priced in USD; FY26 plan assumed ¥140/USD vs ~¥110
  a few years earlier; sensitivity ~¥400-500M OP per ¥1 move [P/S]. Capcom publishes no clean
  constant-FX figure, so part of the yen revenue ramp is translation, not organic — [gap].
- SURPLUS CASH (Graham): net cash ~¥160B, growing faster than it is returned; ~35% payout plus
  ad hoc buybacks; ROE ~22% vs ~7.3% cost of equity. Retaining cash is defensible IF reinvested
  at high returns, but the idle-capital question is real.
- GOVERNANCE: Tsujimoto founding family retains effective control ~22-23% [S, aggregators —
  VERIFY against EDINET 有価証券報告書]; founder Kenzo (Chairman), son Haruhiro (President/COO);
  one-share-one-vote. Cross-holdings (政策保有株式) not yet checked in the yuho — [gap].
- CAPCOM'S OWN RISK BOX names the whole bear case [P, Integrated Report 2025]: "Dependence on
  popular series and shortage of hit titles"; "Declining IP value due to brand saturation in
  long-running series"; "Rising development costs with the emergence of new technologies";
  AI/copyright brand risk; and "as barriers to entry fall, the stability of the industry as a
  whole may be shaken." This directly pressure-tests the "cheap-to-build competitors don't
  matter" claim — the company itself concedes falling barriers can shake industry stability.
- RISING DEV COST: R&D ¥49,496M FY25 (+15%, ~1.9x a decade ago), ~90% to Digital Contents;
  per-title cost "trending upwards"; headcount 3,766 (2,846 developers). Bigger budgets raise
  the stakes of any single flop.

## Valuation and the "US multiple" question [S, aggregators — verify]

- Capcom: P/E ~25.5x TTM / ~22.4x fwd; EV/EBIT ~17x; P/B ~5.2x; op margin ~38.5%; ROE ~22%;
  ROIC ~57%; yield ~1.4%. Highest operating margin in the peer set.
- Peers (Jul 6-7, 2026): EA fwd P/E ~23.2x (op margin ~15%, ROE ~13.5%); TTWO unprofitable
  (GTA VI pre-launch), fwd ~38x is meaningless on negative trailing; Nintendo P/E ~19x (op
  margin ~16%); Square Enix ~31x TTM (trough ROE ~8.7%); Bandai Namco ~18x; Konami ~25x (op
  margin ~27.5%); Sega Sammy low-quality/low-multiple.
- COMPARATIVE READ [S]: Capcom already trades at the HIGH end of Japanese peers (above Nintendo
  ~19x and Bandai Namco ~18x, near Konami ~25x) and roughly LEVEL with EA on forward earnings
  (~22.4x vs ~23.2x) despite far superior margins and returns. Its multiple has ALSO re-rated
  up over the decade (mid-teens 2015-2020 → mid-20s/low-30s 2023-2025). So the "would trade at
  a much higher multiple if US-listed / Japan discount" premise is NOT cleanly supported by the
  numbers — present the peer table as fact and pose the question, don't assert the discount.

## The lens [P unless noted]

- Buffett 1989 letter: "It's far better to buy a wonderful company at a fair price than a fair
  company at a wonderful price." + "time is the friend of the wonderful business, the enemy of
  the mediocre" [re-verify adjacent wording].
- Buffett 2007 letter: "A truly great business must have an enduring 'moat' that protects
  excellent returns on invested capital..." + the low-cost-producer / world-wide-brand test;
  See's Candies as the high-return, low-capital-intensity, pricing-power ideal.
- Graham, Intelligent Investor Ch. 20: "MARGIN OF SAFETY" — quality does not excuse
  overpayment; price must leave a cushion. This is the tension with Buffett's "fair price."
- Synthesis questions to POSE (not answer): wonderful business or well-run hit-driven publisher?
  moat widening or narrowing? what does the Japan-vs-US gap actually measure (quality, governance
  /capital-allocation, or mispricing)? what would a private owner pay for this record vs ~22x
  forward after a decade of re-rating, and where is the margin of safety at that price?
