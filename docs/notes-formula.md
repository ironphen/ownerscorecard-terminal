# The formula: what we offer, in what form, under what discipline

*Drafted 2026-07-21; revised same day after a 30-agent adversarial red-team (wf_54154823)
killed the first version's paid tier — no payable unit, no relevance mechanism, a covenant
built to be breached, and a paywalled accountability letter. This version answers each kill.
It binds us, not the reader. Sections marked OPEN await Ryan's ratification.*

## The offer

| Layer | What it is | Price |
|---|---|---|
| The terminal | The verified records: company pages, groupings, almanac, flags | Free, forever |
| The wire | Daily filings, translated; the coming-due band; day permalinks | Free, forever |
| The owners' area | Votes, pay, meetings: the ownership record | Free, forever |
| The ledger | Every dated conclusion and abstention, without self-grading | Free, forever |
| The annual letter | The year's ledger narrated; corrections; member questions | Free, forever |
| The notes (forms 1–3 below) | The reading of the records, and its maintenance | Paid at publication; priced notes roll free after N months (OPEN: N) |

The free layers earn the trust. The letter is free because accountability that must be paid
for to be checked is not accountability, and because the model for its anatomy is free to
the world. What the subscriber pays for is narrower and stronger than the first draft had
it: the readings, and the maintenance of the readings.

## What renewal buys

The red-team's central finding, adopted: prose alone is not a payable unit when the records
under it are free. The payable unit is the maintained library.

Every company note ends with named watch items: the specific filed items that would change
the picture. Those items enter the terminal's machinery. When the named filing lands, the
wire's existing coming-due and filing surfaces discharge the notification automatically,
backlinked to the note that named it, and when the landed filing actually changes the
picture, a dated answer (form 3) says how. A subscriber's shelf of notes is therefore not
an archive that depreciates; it is a set of live commitments the publication services. The
notes archive is linked from the company pages it reads. Year-two renewal buys the
continued servicing of year-one's readings plus the new ones, which is a reason to renew
that no static library gives.

## The forms

Five forms. Everything published is one of them; the critic pass enforces the
classification, and a draft that fits none is reshaped or not published.

### 1. The company note — the spine

**Cap: 1,800 words** (OPEN: or tighter/looser — the caps are a genuine two-way question).
The record carries the depth; the note carries the reading.

The anatomy is five questions, not five headings. The form gate checks that each question
is answered in the business's own economic terms, not that a heading appears:

1. **The record.** What ten years of filings show, from the company's page, linked.
2. **Why does the money keep choosing this business?** Premium, cost position, spread, or
   float, in the business's own terms: cost of float for insurers, spread net of credit
   cost for banks, the payout mandate framing for REITs, cost-and-reserves position for
   price-takers. For businesses selling to consumers, the payment-verdict test: proof the
   customer had a free or cheaper alternative and paid anyway.
3. **What does a marginal retained-or-distributed dollar earn?** Retention arithmetic the
   reader can check (Buffett 1984, 1992). Issuer-repurchase tables are filed record:
   dollars spent, shares retired, average price paid may be quoted as arithmetic. The rule
   is no current market price and no verdict on any price, past or present; whether
   repurchase prices sat below value is a conclusion only form 2 may draw.
4. **The stewardship.** Ownership, board, pay against the record, the votes. The mission
   lives here: reading the alignment that exists.
5. **What an owner watches.** The named watch items that enter the maintenance machinery
   above.

Plus one requirement no template can pre-name: **the bespoke section the record itself
demands** — the Capcom note's "machines," the section a generic anatomy would never
predict. The critic pass carries a density gate: the bespoke section and question 2 must
assert things not computable from the free page. That section is the note's reason to
exist and the part no subscriber's machine can generate from our own free data.

No valuation. Positions footer on every note.

**The docket principle** (stated in advance, like any test): company notes follow the
filing season crossed with the terminal's own evidence of what its readers hold and read —
most-read pages, most-held shelves — plus member coverage requests. Relevance is designed,
not accidental.

### 2. The priced examination — the ledger's feeder

**Cap: 2,400 words, filed-record tables excluded from the count.** The subject may be a
company, a cohort, or a market; "Weeds and Flowers" (a market) and the queued alcohol-shelf
cohort both live here. Side-by-side per-criterion presentation of filed records is licensed
procedure, per the almanac's precedent; ordering companies by overall merit is the
forbidden pronouncement.

Published when filings and prices together force a conclusion or a recorded abstention;
both outcomes enter the ledger dated. The commitment to CONCLUDE remains zero. But the
commitment to sit the examination is not: **at least one priced examination per quarter**,
ending in a conclusion or a dated abstention, so no subscriber year contains zero acts of
judgment. The ledger row (date, price that day, one-sentence conclusion, the tape since) is
free by ratified doctrine; the reasoning is paid and rolls free after N months (OPEN: N).
Never a target, never an estimate, never a rating.

### 3. The dated answer — the maintenance made visible

**Cap: 400 words.** Published when a filing lands on a prior note's watch items and changes
the picture; links the original note; enters the ledger only if priced. Routine landings
that change nothing are discharged by the wire's backlink alone. This is the form that
makes the follow-up commitments of form 1 deliverable instead of debt.

### 4. The letter — free, one per year

**Cap: 3,000 words. Late February, on the Buffett precedent** (OPEN: date), on the prior
year's closed ledger: the ledger restated without self-grading, corrections at the foot
(corrections also post to the free ledger as they occur, never waiting for the calendar),
what the publication got wrong in method, what changed in the product, member questions
answered by name. The year's best conversion event, free precisely because of what it is.

### 5. The essay — free, occasional, method only

The boundary is a test, enforced in the critic pass: an essay may not read any single
company's current record and may not generate a ledger entry. A draft that does either is
reclassified as a note and inherits that form's cap and price. Essays are the publication
thinking in public about method and markets; company-level expeditions can only publish in
the paid forms. The five-hours material, if it publishes, publishes here.

## Cadence

**The floor: one company note per month, plus the quarterly priced examination, plus the
letter.** Most months should carry two company notes; that sentence is description, not
covenant. The red-team's finding, adopted: a publication whose moat is kept promises never
publishes a cadence it has not already demonstrated. So the sequence is:

1. August–December 2026: run the target rotation in public without publishing a number,
   logging founder-hours per draft, declines included.
2. Ratification of any cadence number requires a banked reserve of at least two approved
   company notes first — the no-price doctrine makes form 1 the only stockpileable form,
   and the buffer converts founder variance into a non-event.
3. The founding-member offer at the kill-gate quotes the demonstrated record ("N notes
   since August, every one dated on the ledger"), never a promised volume.

September and December, the rotation's light months, pre-bank the following earnings
season. The seasonal weighting otherwise stands: earnings seasons carry company notes;
proxy season weights stewardship; the letter lands after the year closes.

## Membership, besides the notes

The kill-gate already tests membership behavior (25–50 founding members prepaying); the
product should match the test. Three mechanics, all doctrine-clean: a named founding-member
roll for those who choose to be named; member questions answered by name in the annual
letter; member coverage requests as an input to the docket principle. (His community-design
edge is the long-run moat here; these are only the mechanics the paid tier needs on day
one.)

## Price

OPEN (phase-2 plan §10), with the red-team's discipline adopted: the reference row is
annotated so volume parity cannot masquerade as price justification. Lyn Alden's $249 buys
model portfolios, a top-12 list, and per-issue personal position disclosure; Grant's
~$1,300 buys priced verdicts on a forty-year record. All of that is on our forbidden list.
Our price therefore attaches to what the comps do not sell: the maintained library, the
quarterly examination record, and the free-forever terminal's trust — and it is set against
retention and founding-member prepay evidence at the kill-gate, not against anyone's issue
count.

## What is never in a note

No ratings, no price targets, no estimates, no model portfolios, no performance claims, no
announced positions, no "we told you so." The ledger counts; it does not grade itself.
Side-by-side per-criterion presentation of filed records is licensed; ordering companies by
overall merit is not.

## Enforcement

The loop, not intention: the critic pass gains a form gate (which form, is each question
answered in the business's own terms, does the essay boundary hold), a density gate
(bespoke section and question 2 must exceed the free page), a citation-verification gate
(so Ryan's read spends on judgment, not fact-checking), and the caps. Caps and forms are
published on /docs only after the August–December run demonstrates them.
